    🔎 Evidence behind the guess

    Smaller distance means a closer match. 0 means exactly the same inputs. The closest example supplies the label; equally close examples with different labels produce a tie.

    Labeled pizzaLabelDistanceEvidence

    Distance = topping difference ÷ 6 + cheese difference ÷ 2. Cheese uses low = 0, medium = 1, high = 2. Scores are rounded to two decimals; they are distances, not confidence percentages.

    How does it learn?

    The inputs are the number of vegetable toppings and amount of cheese. The labels are Order and Skip.

    This classifier remembers your labeled examples. For a new pizza, it finds the closest example and borrows its label. This is called nearest neighbor. Adding or changing an example can change its prediction!

    To compare fairly, toppings (0–6) and cheese (low = 0, medium = 1, high = 2) are each scaled to 0–1. We add their absolute differences. If equally close examples disagree, the classifier asks for another label. Two examples are only a start: this is a guess about taste, not a rule about all pizzas.
